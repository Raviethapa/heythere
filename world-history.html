02 / The history roomPhase one · India

India, across time.

History is not a straight line of kings and dates. It is the changing story of people, institutions, ideas, trade, belief, conflict and everyday life.

This first draft builds an India-focused framework from early urban societies to the republic. It is a map for further reading, not a complete account; many periods require regional histories and competing interpretations. The world-history expansion comes after this foundation.

A working timeline

India is many histories.

Use the periods as starting points; dates and labels can vary by region and scholarly framework.

c. 7000–1900 BCE
01 / Early settlements & Indus cities

From village life to urban networks

Explore Mehrgarh, Harappa and Mohenjo-daro; settlement patterns, craft production, trade, water systems and the still-undeciphered Indus script. Avoid treating the civilisation as a fully understood single political state.

Ask: What counts as evidence?Read next: archaeology & material culture
c. 1500–600 BCE
02 / Vedic & early historic worlds

Texts, ritual, settlement and debate

Study the Vedic traditions alongside archaeology and the emergence of new political formations. Treat literary texts as sources that need context, not as transparent records of every social reality.

Ask: Who produced the source?Read next: Vedic literature and archaeology
c. 600–200 BCE
03 / Mahajanapadas, Buddhism & Jainism

New states and new questions

Explore urbanisation, coinage, trade, philosophical debate, Buddhism, Jainism and the rise of Magadha. Compare religious texts with inscriptions and archaeological evidence.

Ask: How do ideas travel?Read next: early Buddhism, Jainism and state formation
c. 322 BCE–600 CE
04 / Maurya, post-Maurya & Gupta worlds

Empires, regions and exchange

Study Ashoka's inscriptions, administration, long-distance trade, the spread of religions, and varied regional cultures. “Golden age” is a useful debate to examine, not a neutral label for every community.

Ask: Whose prosperity?Read next: inscriptions, trade routes and regional histories
c. 600–1526 CE
05 / Regional kingdoms & Sultanates

Many centres of power

Explore Pallavas, Cholas, Rajputs, Deccan polities, Bhakti and Sufi traditions, the Delhi Sultanate, temple economies, maritime networks and regional languages. Avoid flattening centuries into one narrative.

Ask: How did local and global connect?Read next: regional history and cultural exchange
1526–1757 / 1857
06 / Mughal and early modern India

Courts, countryside and commerce

Study Mughal political culture, revenue systems, regional powers, the Marathas, Sikhs, Rajputs, Deccan states, European trading companies and the changing balance of power.

Ask: How was power negotiated?Read next: regional archives and economic history
1757–1947
07 / Colonial rule & independence

Empire, resistance and competing futures

Trace East India Company expansion, colonial administration, economic change, social reform, 1857, anti-colonial movements, Gandhi and other currents, revolutionary politics, Partition and independence. Include regional, Dalit, Adivasi, labour and women’s perspectives.

Ask: Who gets remembered?Read next: multiple histories of 1857, nationalism and Partition
1947–present
08 / The republic

Constitution, democracy and change

Explore the Constitution, integration of princely states, linguistic reorganisation, planning, wars, social movements, Emergency, economic reforms, federalism, and ongoing debates about citizenship and institutions.

Ask: What changed, and for whom?Read next: constitutional history and oral histories
Phase two · Coming later

Then, the wider world.

After the India foundation, extend the timeline to Mesopotamia, Egypt, China, Greece and Rome, African kingdoms, Islamic empires, the Americas, the Mongol world, maritime trade, colonialism, revolutions and the modern global order — without treating Europe as the only centre of history.

The method

Stay curious.
Distrust easy stories.

The past deserves more than one source and more than one point of view.
