Poetry & lines.

Words for the things we feel before we know how to explain them.

This first notebook starts with Indian voices — Hindi, Urdu, Bengali and Indian English — then makes room for world poetry later. It balances literary context with short quotations and original reflections. Modern poems are not reproduced at length; where a line is quoted, its author and source are identified.

A few doors into poetry

Start with the voice.

These are entry points, not a definitive canon. Read the whole poem or collection where possible.

Hindi / Sant poetry

Direct, questioning verse that challenges ritual certainty and brings spiritual inquiry back to ordinary life.

“The river that flows in you also flows in me.”
A paraphrase of Kabir’s recurring imagery, not a verbatim quotation.Poet profile ↗
Braj / Rajasthani traditions

Devotion, longing and the courage to choose an inner allegiance over social expectation.

“Love can become a way of refusing fear.”
Original reflection inspired by themes in Mirabai’s devotional songs.Poet profile ↗
Urdu

Wit, longing, doubt and the complicated mind — a poet for when simple answers feel suspicious.

“Hazaaron khwahishen aisi…”
Opening words of a famous Ghalib couplet; read the full ghazal in a trusted edition.Poet profile ↗
Urdu

Love and political conscience held together — tenderness without indifference to injustice.

“Bol, ke lab azaad hain tere.”
Short excerpt from Faiz’s poem “Bol”; source the complete text from an authorised edition.Poet profile ↗
Hindi

Interior life, solitude, compassion and a quiet but unmistakable intellectual independence.

Let tenderness be a form of strength.
Original reflection, not a quotation.Sahitya Akademi ↗
Hindi

Public language, disquiet and the urge to speak plainly when institutions stop listening.
