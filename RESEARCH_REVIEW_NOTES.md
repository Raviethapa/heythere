# hey, ravie. — Research review notes (draft, 14 October 2026)

## Scope
Three standalone pages were drafted to fit the existing website. They load the existing `style.css` and use the same Instrument Serif / Hanken Grotesk typography and dark/light theme. Only the relevant navigation destinations in `index.html` and `about.html` were updated; no global style tokens were changed.

## 1. Books & authors
- `books-data.js` contains 100 entries: 50 Hindi-language titles and 50 English-language titles.
- The selection is tailored to interests visible in prior conversation: Indian/world history, literature and poetry, philosophy/reflection, behaviour and habits, personal finance, technology and systems thinking. *The Alchemist* and *Atomic Habits* are included because these were the only titles verifiable in prior notes; reading status is deliberately unset.
- The list is a curated starting point, not a claim that every book has been read, liked, or individually verified against a specific edition. Hindi edition/translation availability may vary; check title/translator/edition before buying.
- Selection anchors include National Book Trust India's Indian literature catalogue; Sahitya Akademi award listings; Booker Prize pages for Indian novels; Nobel Prize bibliography for Tagore; and Project Gutenberg for public-domain Tagore works.

## 2. World history (India-first phase)
- The page starts with an India-focused chronology: early settlements/Indus, Vedic and early historic worlds, Mahajanapadas/Buddhism/Jainism, Maurya/post-Maurya/Gupta, regional kingdoms/Sultanates, Mughal and early modern India, colonial rule/independence, and the republic.
- Dates are approximate where indicated. Broad periodisation is an entry point, not a substitute for regional histories or historiographical debate. The page explicitly encourages comparison of material evidence, inscriptions, texts, oral histories and perspectives often omitted from national narratives.
- World history is identified as phase two, not filled with a shallow global timeline in this first pass.

## 3. Poetry & lines
- Indian poets are the starting point: Kabir, Mirabai, Ghalib, Faiz, Mahadevi Verma, Dushyant Kumar, Dinkar, Tagore, A. K. Ramanujan, Kamala Das, Gulzar and Muktibodh.
- Quote content is deliberately short. Several cards use original reflections rather than attributing invented lines to poets. The Ghalib and Faiz examples are short openings/excerpts; Tagore's “Where the mind is without fear…” is a short opening phrase. The Kabir card is explicitly marked as a paraphrase, not a quotation.
- Before public launch, verify exact wording, transliteration and edition for all quoted excerpts. Do not add long modern poems, song lyrics, or copyrighted translations without permission.
